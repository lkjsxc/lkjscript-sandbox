 HOW TO PLAY

Make everyday trips easier.

  1. Observe. Start traffic. Tap a resident, road or home to see a journey and the reason for a wait.
  2. Connect. Roads carry cars and walkers. Avenues have more lanes. Footpaths offer walking shortcuts. Draw a road or footpath from dry bank to dry bank to build a bridge. New bridges may cross anywhere; their full price appears while dragging.
  3. Bring places closer. Homes add eight people; workplaces offer sixteen jobs. Nearby jobs, shops and parks can replace long drives with short walks.
  4. Offer rail. Build track with bends, place walkable stations, then connect stations with a service. Suspend an empty service to add more stops. People choose it when the whole journey is quicker. Each train holds sixteen people; each departure costs funds. Tap the line to inspect or suspend service.
  5. Balance the budget. Work creates business revenue and wages. Residents buy from local shops and pay fares. Taxes and fares fund construction and operation; the Funds button explains the flow.
  6. Grow. Complete visits, meet the population target and keep trips reliable to earn home permits. Each expansion doubles the next goal.

Stuck? Inspect a queue. A full exit needs more space or another route; a missing destination needs a connection or nearby place. Change junction controls to balance flows. Remove lets you review a rectangle, including occupied roads, before committing.

Drag to pan; pinch or scroll to zoom. Roads draw straight lines, Remove draws rectangles up to 256 tiles. A second finger cancels a stroke. Keyboard: arrows, +/−, F to fit, Space to start/pause, Escape to cancel or return.

 MONEY IN YOUR CITY

Every journey has a purpose.

Work brings outside revenue to businesses. Wages go to residents; payroll and sales taxes go to the city. Shopping and leisure move household money to local destinations. Boarding costs $1; residents with no savings receive a free ride.

Road and track upkeep is due every 64 cycles. Trains charge per departure. Unpaid upkeep carries forward without interest; trains finish paid legs before waiting for funds. New households bring $24 each in outside capital.
